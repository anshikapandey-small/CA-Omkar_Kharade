Acts & Rules Guide

Every filing you make is based on a law. This section lists the main Acts in plain language for clients in Virar, Nalasopara, Vasai, Palghar and Naigaon.

Main Laws

  • Income-tax law: taxes on income, with rules for returns, TDS and assessments. Check which Act and rules apply to the tax year in question.
  • CGST Act, 2017 and IGST Act, 2017: govern GST registration, returns and input tax credit
  • Companies Act, 2013: incorporation, audit and annual filings
  • LLP Act, 2008: registration and compliance for LLPs

Why Rules Matter

Penalties and interest follow the section that applies, so knowing the right provision decides whether you owe a small fee or a large demand. Contact us if you are unsure which rule applies.

Income-tax Law: Returns, TDS, Assessments and Notices

Income-tax law decides who must file a return, how income is classified, which deductions are allowed, when tax must be deducted at source (TDS) and how assessments, notices and appeals work. India is moving to the Income-tax Act, 2025 for tax years starting 1 April 2026, while the Income-tax Act, 1961 continues to govern earlier years. That means an old return, a pending notice or an assessment for a past year is still handled under the 1961 Act, and a current-year return follows the new Act. Always check which Act and which rules apply to the year in question before acting. If you need help with a return or a notice, see our income tax return filing service.

GST Laws: CGST, SGST/UTGST and IGST

GST is governed by the CGST Act, 2017, the State GST or UT GST Acts and the IGST Act, 2017, along with detailed rules and a steady stream of notifications and circulars. These laws cover registration, the time and place of supply, invoicing, input tax credit, returns such as GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, refunds, audits, notices and penalties. A mistake in classifying a supply or claiming credit can create tax, interest and penalty at the same time, so it helps to know the relevant section. Our GST registration and return filing team explains what applies to your business.

Companies Act, 2013 and LLP Act, 2008

The Companies Act, 2013 governs incorporation, directors, share capital, board meetings, audit, annual returns and the penalties for default. The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 does the same for LLPs, with lighter requirements. Both Acts require regular filings with the Registrar of Companies through the MCA portal, and missing them leads to additional fees and, for continued default, director disqualification. Read more on our company registration and ROC compliance page.

Other Laws That Affect Small Businesses

Many owners in Virar, Nalasopara, Vasai and Palghar also deal with laws outside tax and company law. Examples include Professional Tax and the Shops and Establishments registration in Maharashtra, Provident Fund and ESI for employees, and the MSMED Act, which affects how quickly micro and small enterprises must be paid. Income-tax law also links to that Act: payments to registered micro and small suppliers beyond the permitted period can lead to a disallowance of the expense. Ask us if you are unsure whether a particular law applies to you.

How to Find the Right Section

Start with the document in front of you. A notice usually quotes a section, a return form quotes the year, and a GST demand quotes a financial year and a section of the CGST Act. Note that section, confirm the year, and check the latest Finance Act amendments and notifications. Then read the relevant rule and any circular that explains it. Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I read the Acts?

The official texts are published on the India Code website and the relevant department sites.

Do the rules change often?

Yes, mainly through the annual Finance Act and notifications, so verify current provisions.

Which Act applies to my older income tax return or notice?

Returns, assessments and notices for earlier years continue under the law that applied to those years, so send us the notice or return and we will confirm the correct provisions.

What is the difference between an Act, a Rule and a notification?

An Act is the main law passed by Parliament. Rules explain how the Act is applied, and notifications and circulars change dates, limits or procedures. All three can affect your compliance.

Do you explain legal sections in plain language?

Yes. Send us the section number or the notice and we explain what it means, what it costs and what you need to do.
