Who Needs an Audit?

Audit becomes mandatory depending on your entity type, turnover and the law that applies. Companies generally need a statutory audit every year. Businesses and professionals above prescribed limits need a tax audit. Even when audit is not mandatory, many owners choose a voluntary review before applying for a bank loan or bringing in partners.

Our Approach

We understand your business, plan the audit, test transactions on a sample basis, and discuss findings with you before finalising the report. You get practical suggestions along with the report, so the audit adds value beyond compliance.

Statutory Audit for Companies and LLPs

Companies must appoint an auditor and have their accounts audited every year. An LLP needs an audit when its turnover or contribution crosses the limits in the LLP rules. A statutory audit checks whether the financial statements give a true and fair view and whether the books follow the applicable accounting standards. Banks, investors and the Registrar of Companies rely on this report. We also coordinate the related filings, as described on our company registration and ROC compliance page.

Tax Audit for Businesses and Professionals

A tax audit applies to businesses and professionals whose turnover or receipts cross the limits in the Income-tax law, and to some taxpayers who opt out of presumptive taxation. The report is filed electronically and must be completed before the due date for the return. Limits and due dates can change through Finance Acts and notifications, so we confirm them for your case each year. Late audit or a missing report can lead to a penalty, so planning early is important.

Internal Audit, Stock Audit and Bank Audit Support

An internal audit reviews processes such as purchases, inventory, billing and cash handling and points out where money leaks. A stock audit verifies inventory against books for lenders or partners. We also help with bank-required reviews and certificates so your loan application or renewal is not delayed. These services are useful for traders, manufacturers and growing firms across Virar, Nalasopara, Vasai and Palghar.

GST Audit and Reconciliation

GST returns, books and the annual return should agree with each other. We compare GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B with your accounts, find differences in tax paid, input tax credit and turnover, and recommend corrections before the department raises a query. This work is closely linked to our GST registration and return filing service and our accounting and bookkeeping work.

How to Prepare for an Audit

  • Close your books and reconcile bank statements before the audit starts.
  • Keep invoices, vouchers, loan statements and TDS and GST records in order.
  • Prepare lists of debtors, creditors, fixed assets and inventory.
  • Share minutes, agreements and earlier audit reports.

Well-kept records shorten the audit and reduce fees. We also advise you on gaps to fix well before the next year-end.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is a tax audit required?

It depends on your turnover or receipts, the nature of your income and the limits in force for the year. Contact us and we will confirm for your case.

How long does an audit take?

It depends on size and how organised your records are. Clean books reduce time and cost.

What is the difference between a statutory audit and a tax audit?

A statutory audit is required by company or LLP law and reports on the financial statements. A tax audit is required by the Income-tax law for taxpayers above specified limits and reports on tax-related particulars.

Is an audit needed if my turnover is small?

Not always. It depends on your entity type, turnover, profit and any special rules. Share your details and we will confirm whether an audit is required.

Can an audit help me get a bank loan?

A clean audited balance sheet and a stock or bank-required report often make a loan application stronger, although approval always rests with the lender.
